## 1. The Five Core Content Pillars

To guarantee editorial diversity and prevent audience fatigue across the 30+ generated assets, the Content Engine enforces a 5-pillar taxonomy. Every generated asset must map to exactly one primary pillar.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE 5 CONTENT PILLARS                           │
├─────┬────────────────────────────┬─────────────┬───────────────────────┤
│ #   │ Pillar Name                │ Target Mix  │ Core Editorial Focus  │
├─────┼────────────────────────────┼─────────────┼───────────────────────┤
│ P1  │ Creator Workflow Education │ 25% (8-10)  │ Reducing Fragmentation│
│ P2  │ Hook Writing & Short Video │ 25% (8-10)  │ First-3s Retention    │
│ P3  │ 1-to-Many Transformation   │ 20% (6-8)   │ Output Multiplication │
│ P4  │ Creator Operations & Cadence│ 15% (5-6)  │ Systems Over Hustle   │
│ P5  │ Attribution & Experiments  │ 15% (5-6)   │ Measurable Outcomes   │
└─────┴────────────────────────────┴─────────────┴───────────────────────┘
```

### Pillar 1: Creator Workflow Education (Addressing Tool Fragmentation)
* **Audience Problem:** Creators and editors report operational fatigue from constantly switching between disparate single-purpose tools to transcribe, clip, resize, and publish (EV-006).
* **Editorial Objective:** Educate the market on how consolidated workflows simplify video production and reduce operational friction.
* **Three Example Post Concepts:**
  1. *Concept 1A (Carousel - LinkedIn):* "The Disconnected Multi-Tool Stack Teardown" — Visualizing the operational complexity of managing 5+ single-purpose utilities vs. a unified repurposing workflow.
  2. *Concept 1B (Short Video - Shorts/Reels):* "Transcript-Driven Video Editing" — 40s visual demo showing how deleting a sentence in a text transcript removes the take from the video timeline (EV-002).
  3. *Concept 1C (Thread - X):* "Signs your content workflow has an operational bottleneck (and the 3 habits that streamline it)."
* **Preferred Platforms & Formats:** LinkedIn Carousels (4:5), YouTube Shorts (9:16), X Threads.
* **Suitable Hooks & CTAs:**
  * *Hook:* *"If you need 5 different tools open just to publish one video, your workflow has a bottleneck."*
  * *CTA:* *"Explore IncuBrix Creator Studio: [`https://app.incubrix.com/`](file:///c:/Users/Harini%20V/OneDrive/Desktop/incu/research/snapshots/app_incubrix_com.html) (EV-004)."*
* **Supporting Evidence Required:** EV-002 (IncuBrix Advertised Superpowers), EV-003 (Pricing Tiers starting with Free exploration plan), EV-006 (Observed creator pain points in public posts).
* **Claims to Avoid Until Verified:** Do NOT claim "saves exactly 8–10 hours weekly" or "cuts editing time by 82%" as facts. Frame benefits around feature capabilities (e.g., "consolidates transcription, editing, and scheduling into one interface").

### Pillar 2: Hook Writing & Short-Video Tutorials (First-3-Second Retention)
* **Audience Problem:** High swipe-away rates on vertical feeds. Creators spend hours on valuable deep-dives, but viewers swipe away early due to slow, conversational introductions.
* **Editorial Objective:** Teach repeatable hook structures (contrarian statements, pattern interrupts, immediate proof) that hold viewer attention in the opening 3 seconds.
* **Three Example Post Concepts:**
  1. *Concept 2A (Short Video - Shorts/TikTok):* "The First 3 Seconds" — Contrasting a slow conversational opening with an immediate kinetic disruption hook.
  2. *Concept 2B (Carousel - LinkedIn/Instagram):* "7 Hook Archetypes for Educational Content" — Step-by-step deconstruction of high-retention opening lines.
  3. *Concept 2C (Post - LinkedIn/X):* "The 3 Elements of an Educational Hook: Curiosity + Context + Clarity."
* **Preferred Platforms & Formats:** YouTube Shorts (9:16), TikTok/Reels (9:16), LinkedIn Carousels.
* **Suitable Hooks & CTAs:**
  * *Hook:* *"Stop opening educational videos with 'Hey guys, today I want to talk about...' Try this opening instead:"*
  * *CTA:* *"Save this post for your next script."*
* **Supporting Evidence Required:** EV-008 (OpusClip audit showing high engagement on creator pain hooks), EV-006 (IncuBrix post: "Attention doesn't depend on a face").
* **Claims to Avoid Until Verified:** Do NOT promise guaranteed view counts or claim that a specific hook formula will achieve a fixed retention rate. Frame as creative best practices for testing.

## 2. Brand Voice Rules & Guardrails

To ensure professional integrity and defensibility during placement interviews:

### 2.1. Tone, Vocabulary & Sentence Structure
* **Tone:** Professional, direct, practitioner-oriented, and evidence-grounded. Reads like an experienced growth engineer or content strategist.
* **Vocabulary:**
  * *Approved Terms:* Content Repurposing, Creator Operations, Feed Retention, Pacing, Kinetic Subtitles, Programmatic Tracking, Scheduling Export.
  * *Banned AI Slop & Exaggerated Buzzwords:* "Game-changer", "Revolutionary", "Skyrocket", "Unlock your potential", "Magic", "Secret sauce", "Supercharge", "Unleash".
* **Sentence Length & Readability:**
  * Average sentence length: 12 to 18 words.
  * Readability: Plain language, active voice, avoiding unnecessary jargon.
  * Formatting: Regular line breaks and short paragraphs (2–3 sentences max) to ensure readability on mobile feeds.

### 2.2. Hook Standards
* **Approved Hook Styles:**
  1. *Contrarian Observation:* "Stop doing [common practice]. Here is why it often fails on vertical feeds:"
  2. *Direct Workflow Contrast:* "How 1 recording converts into multiple platform-native drafts."
  3. *Operational Question:* "How much of your weekly schedule is spent on manual editing versus creative planning?"
* **Prohibited Hooks:** Clickbait promises ("This 1 secret will make you viral"), artificial urgency ("Act now or get left behind"), or ungrounded superlative assertions.

## 3. Strict Claim-Verification Policy

To prevent the engine from producing unverified marketing copy, every factual claim is governed by four distinct categories:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      CLAIM-VERIFICATION TAXONOMY                       │
├──────────┬─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ Category │ Classification              │ Engine Generation Rule        │
├──────────┼─────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
│ Cat 1    │ Verified Factual Claims     │ Permitted as factual copy     │
│ Cat 2    │ Advertised (Untested)       │ Framed as advertised features │
│ Cat 3    │ Proposed Hypotheses/Targets │ Explicitly labeled as targets │
│ Cat 4    │ Unsupported / Fabricated    │ STRICTLY BLOCKED BY ENGINE    │
└──────────┴─────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘
```

### Category 1: Verified Factual Claims (Permitted as Factual Statements)
* **Definition:** Facts with direct, traceable proof in the evidence register (`evidence_register.csv`).
* **Approved Facts:**
  * IncuBrix operates at `incubrix.com`, `incubrix.ai`, and `app.incubrix.com` (EV-001).
  * IncuBrix lists 6 public pricing tiers: Free (\$0), Starter (\$10/mo), Essential (\$20/mo), Growth (\$50/mo), Scale (\$100/mo), and Pro (\$250/mo) (EV-003).
  * IncuBrix states that it offers a Free plan for exploring the platform, with no free trials for paid plans (EV-003).
  * IncuBrix's stated core mission is turning one content into clips, captions, voiceovers, and posts for every platform (EV-001).
  * IncuBrix has 1,235 followers on LinkedIn as of October 29, 2026 (EV-005).

### Category 2: Publicly Advertised Features (Untested in Product)
* **Definition:** Capabilities advertised on public marketing pages but not tested via an authenticated session.
* **Mandatory Rule:** Cannot be described as personally tested, proven, or demonstrated.
* **Framing Standard:** Must be introduced with transparent framing: *"As advertised on IncuBrix's public website..."* or *"According to IncuBrix's Creator Studio specifications..."*.
* **Examples:** Text transcript editing, voice cloning, direct scheduling to Bluesky/Spotify (EV-002).
* **Source Material Policy:** Long-form source documents (e.g., sample case studies or webinar transcripts) must NOT be silently treated as proof of an IncuBrix product capability.

### Category 3: Proposed Hypotheses, Targets & Roadmap Items
* **Definition:** Performance goals, metric projections, and operational hypotheses.
* **Mandatory Rule:** Must be explicitly labeled as hypotheses or targets: *"Hypothesized target: $\ge 45\%$ retention"* or *"Proposed measurement framework"*.
* **Rule on Competitor/Stack Modeling:** Theoretical stack costs (e.g., estimating multiple tools at \$74/mo or \$180/mo) and time estimates (e.g., 8–10 hours) are conceptual models and must be explicitly noted as unverified illustrative hypotheses.

### Category 4: Unsupported & Fabricated Claims (STRICTLY BLOCKED)
* **The Engine's Automated Blocklist:**
  1. Fabricated survey results or statistical claims (e.g., claiming "$N=24$, $p < 0.01$, beating industry benchmarks by 2.8x" without an actual survey).
  2. Invented competitor benchmarks or ungrounded performance multipliers.
  3. Fictitious client names, company case studies, or user testimonials (e.g., inventing "ScaleMedia Labs").
  4. Unverified offers (e.g., "Upload 1 video, get 10 clips free", "no credit card required", or unverified money-back guarantees).
  5. Misleading urgency or false scarcity.
* **Automated Enforcement:** The engine's guardrail module will scan all generated drafts and reject any asset containing Category 4 violations.
